## 00:01:46–00:05:35  [talking-head]
**Spoken:** Matthew's two main use cases: "really keeping myself sane and reducing the context switching." Like many, he landed on a chief-of-staff agent paradigm — but the unlock that takes it past just "who I talk to to get anything done" is that he gave his chief of staff access to all his inboxes — "not just a couple, but truly everything": all email addresses, calendars, even different chat accounts, across personal and professional use cases, plus Slack. Number-one job today: distill all of it — what are tasks, what are FYIs — then get stuck into the tasks. His differentiator: the chief of staff "can handle the whole end-to-end — I call it a self-completing task list": it's tapped into all inboxes, receives what's coming in regularly, determines what subset is tasks, and identifies which of his other specialist bots are best positioned to take a first stab. Example from his job: territory planning and territory disputes — his team is really a combined RevOps/MarOps (revenue operations + marketing operations). Growing a massive business means hiring salespeople who own territories; a rep meets someone at an event, then realizes a colleague owns that account — a territory dispute. Tickets arrive saying "I don't know if I should work this or not." In the past that took human judgment pointing at rules and processes ("you should have clicked that button, not this button"); now you can, "in a much more automated way, understand what's really going on here, how does this align with our rules of engagement, and what's the policy moving forward." Roshan: what about sales ops/marketing ops for a three-person company with a single game on a website?
