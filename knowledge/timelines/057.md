## 00:03:30–00:08:25  [talking-head]
**Spoken:** Shubh — the rest of the end-to-end customer journey: "Part two is after the call — we want to go end to end, not stop there. The same bot compounds for me: after every call it reviews the Granola transcript and figures out what that person resonated with or didn't. Arm your teams with it: have your bot look at every transcript — if I talked about a note-taking feature they didn't care about and they said so on the call, the next prep document won't mention that feature unless it's really relevant. Give it two or three weeks and it becomes really powerful — you leverage other people's calls, your calls get way better, and you stop tripping over yourself." Part three, **support**: "A lot of customers need support and there aren't enough people to do it one-on-one. A lot of founders leave support to the very last thing — believe me, I've been there — and it makes your life very tough. The most important part that makes it work: spend the time empowering your bot — not only connect it to your data, but to your billing, and to the risky pieces you're scared to give — that's where most support struggle happens." Part four, **activation**: "You know your wow moment — when your customer is activated. How do you decrease time to value? One of the easiest ways is to give people money so they get there. I hooked this bot up: when an activation event happens (demo: a template shared with teammates), we have $1,000 ready to send and it automatically sends that email with credits so the user feels rewarded in that moment. Instead of monitoring every customer journey manually — or building a tool from scratch — offload it to your bot and it runs the campaign on its own." Plus: contracts (bot goes back and forth), outbound pipeline generation, calendar filling. "These all happen autonomously — I no longer need to worry about 99% of this journey. There are cases where I want to step in — why didn't this call resonate — and I spend my time on that, which is actually very enjoyable. I actually enjoy talking to customers; that's why this is emotionally complicated for me — I have to intentionally take some of this back from my bots rather than letting them do all of it. But it's a very cool time: scale to thousands of customers with your bots, and you focus on good decisions, velocity, and focus."
