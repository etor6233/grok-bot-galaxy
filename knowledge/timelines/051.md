## 00:03:10–00:06:10  [talking-head]
**Spoken:** Audience Q: "Can you talk about how bots manage context/memory — how it might overlap across teams or roles?" Presenter: "These act as self-contained agents you can steer and control. Each bot has its own memory, but there's also a **shared memory pool** bots can contribute to — when they and you decide there's something the team should remember. There's a bit of prompting under the hood to tune how bots choose one or the other, but in general bots have an individual memory pool they write to and refine over time. That's why we like the role-based version: you get really strong memories for how to become a really good engineer over time. We're still experimenting — the group-chats interface lets you bring those things together. Multiple approaches work: you could have a single builder bot that is EngProd and Design all in one — that works too, but for more complex use cases separating roles helps." Co-presenter: "We've been trained with agents to worry about context pollution and context management — what does the agent know, do I need to compact, when do I run out of context window. The ultimate goal is to make this incredibly simple and accessible, so people don't spend meta-work thinking about what agents know."
**On screen:** Lessons slide remains; Q&A set.
**Actions:** Panel answers the memory/context question in depth; session winds toward close.
**Facts:**
- Memory architecture: per-bot individual memory + a shared team memory pool bots contribute to by decision; prompting tunes which pool is used
- Role separation gives stronger per-role memories; a single multi-role builder bot also works for simpler cases
- Product goal stated: remove the human's meta-work of context management (compaction, pollution worries) — make it simple and accessible
