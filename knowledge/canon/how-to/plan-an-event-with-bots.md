# Prepare an event plan with specialist bots

Source: Jenny's guest advice at the end of Day 1, 2026-09-15, [061](../../timelines/061.md)–[063](../../timelines/063.md). The hosts dispatched a budget brief; scouting, RFPs, negotiations, and overnight drafts were principally proposed workflows. This is not a completed-event case study or current permit guidance.

## Establish criteria before searching

The guest asked for capacity, timing, city, space type, food, alcohol, kitchen/catering arrangements, preferred vendors, and a budget. The hosts were considering an evening gallery-style event for roughly 100–200 people in San Francisco; those values belonged to the demo. [062, 02:40–06:35](../../timelines/062.md); [063, 00:00–01:50](../../timelines/063.md)

## Split work by responsibility

The planner coordinates specialists in the same way a chief of staff coordinates a team. Jenny recommended establishing market-rate baselines and budget parameters **before** asking a bot to negotiate venue proposals. The hosts kept marketing outside the initial event-logistics budget and requested at least a microphone and speaker. [062, 06:35–09:55](../../timelines/062.md); [063, 00:00–02:53](../../timelines/063.md)

## Distinguish preparation from commitments

The suggested overnight deliverable was researched venues and drafted emails ready in the morning. Sending RFPs, negotiating, and committing money are separate actions that need the user's actual authority and budget boundaries. At [062, 00:00–02:40](../../timelines/062.md), the hosts explicitly said no outreach had happened yet.

Jenny described AI's legal work as a first pass that still needs experienced human review. Earlier stage speculation that a caterer would resolve licensing is not a verified permit rule. Treat event requirements as questions to research for the actual city and venue. [063, 02:53–05:27](../../timelines/063.md); [046, 01:30–02:40](../../timelines/046.md)
