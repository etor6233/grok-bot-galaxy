# Improve a bot system while keeping it lean

Evidence: Day 3, 2026-09-17. These are presenter workflow practices, not measurements of token savings or product pricing.

Give each bot a job, relevant sources, an output format and clear escalation rules. Start with a simple task and one or two tools, then expand after reviewing actual results. Persistent roles can join temporary initiative chats without creating a new permanent bot for every initiative.

Blake's weekly self-improvement routine had two parts:

1. **System audit:** find ignored routines, tasks the human performed manually three or more times, and missing skills. Propose one concrete yes/no improvement; do not implement it until approved.
2. **Voice learning:** compare bot drafts with the messages the human actually sent. Extract changes to openings, tone, length, structure and phrasing; send examples to the writing bot to update its rules.

Only Gus reported the proposed system change. Wally did not independently message the human, and the routine never sent customer email or Slack messages. The one-suggestion limit was deliberate: the earlier audit produced too many proposed bots and routines.

Use these cost-conscious practices from the Q&A:

- Choose a cadence based on the job. Several always-running 15-minute routines can add up to hundreds of checks per day.
- Keep the team specialized and small enough to supervise; avoid adding dozens of bots without distinct work.
- Prefer the available direct-tool path when it can do the task well. Blake described full UI-driven form building as more costly than preparing the content for a form-building tool. The session gave no numeric price comparison.
- Teach quiet conditions as well as alert conditions. Do not create a second inbox of low-value bot updates.
- Ask for a dependency/context audit before reorganizing a mature team. Bot-sprawl cleanup was discussed as a design problem, not a demonstrated automatic repair feature.

For long dictated briefs, first ask the bot to distill the specific facts, tasks and unresolved decisions, then analyze them. Ask it to restate the plan before action. These checks help inspect understanding; they do not guarantee correctness.

An adapted routine brief:

> Once a week, review ignored routines and repeated manual requests. Propose one specific improvement for approval. Compare available drafts with their sent versions and send concrete writing lessons to the voice specialist. Report only the decision I need to make and a short count of learned edits. Never send a customer message as part of this audit.
